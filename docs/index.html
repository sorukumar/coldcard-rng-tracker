The Attacker Evolved

Our Core Attack Waves

The attacker changed their strategy as the community started tracking them. Between Wave 1 and Wave 3, they shifted how they moved the stolen funds.

Here's what happened: In Wave 1, the attacker sent stolen funds to a few shared addresses, which were easy to track. Once the community noticed, the attacker adapted. By Wave 3, they started sending each victim's funds to a brand new, unique address. The only clue left was the network fee: they paid exactly 201.0 sat/vB across 1,626 separate transactions, even when normal fees were around 3 sat/vB. This proves a single automated script was running the attack.
Community-Reported Activity (Waves 5-10): While we focus on the 4 main attack waves, the community has tracked up to 10 waves in total. These later waves are harder to tie directly to the main attacker, but they are widely reported.

How The Funds Were Laundered

Cross-Validated · Chainalysis

Multiple attackers were likely involved. We've confirmed four main ways the stolen funds were laundered.

Type 1: Holding in Cold Storage

Sent to large cold storage wallets. Over $35M is just sitting there unspent.

Type 2: Moved to Other Chains

Moved to Ethereum and other chains, then laundered through TornadoCash.

Type 3: Sent to Exchanges

Sent to crypto exchanges through intermediary wallets.

Type 4: Mixed via CoinJoin

Mixed using Wasabi Wallet to hide the transaction trail.
